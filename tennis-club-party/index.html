How to host a tennis-club party

You don’t need a court, a country club membership or a single forehand. You need a theme you commit to. Here’s how to nail every part of it.

The invite

Lean into the members-only energy. A few lines to steal:

  • “[Name]’s Tennis Club. Members and guests welcome. Whites required.”
  • “Club Thirty-Five: now accepting new members.”
  • “Serving 35. Come watch her take the set.”
  • “Thirty, love.” (For the 30th. In tennis, love means zero, so it’s a score and a sentiment.)
  • “Game, set, forty. Drinks at the clubhouse.”

The dress code

Two schools of thought. Tennis whites are classic and clean, and they make every group photo look like a heritage ad. All-pink is louder, sillier and extremely good on camera. Either way, put it on the invite in bold so nobody shows up in jeans.

Bonus points for visors, sweatbands, pleated skirts and a tiny towel slung over one shoulder. Put the guest of honor in a different color so she pops in every shot.

The tablescape

This is where the theme earns its keep. Start with a centerpiece: a silver trophy bowl (or any shiny bowl you can find) piled high with tennis balls. Add green-and-white striped napkins, or run strips of white tape down a green tablecloth for instant court lines. Mini rackets make great place-card holders.

For the final touch, print the menu like a scorecard: first set (snacks), second set (mains), tiebreak (dessert).

The signature drink

Every club needs a house drink with a pun for a name. Try a Love-All Spritz: sparkling rosé, a splash of pink grapefruit juice, a top-up of soda water and a lime wheel on the rim (the closest thing to a tennis ball you can float in a glass). For the non-drinkers, pour a Deuce: same thing, minus the wine, plus extra fizz.

The playlist

Make it a bit: only songs with “love” in the title. Since love means zero in tennis, the joke writes itself, and someone will figure it out about three songs in.

The games

Match-point trivia. Questions about the guest of honor, scored like tennis: 15, 30, 40, game. Whoever knows her best takes the set.

The trophy toss. Everyone lines up at the “baseline” and tosses tennis balls into the trophy bowl. Winner gets a prize. Loser gives a toast.

Best dressed. Hand out a mini trophy for the crispest whites, or the most committed pink.

Host tip Ask every guest to bring one tennis ball. Hand out markers, have everyone write a birthday message on theirs, and drop them all into the trophy bowl. Instant keepsake, zero extra budget.

The bachelorette version: her last singles match

The tennis bachelorette is the same party with a better storyline. She’s retiring from singles. She’s moving on to doubles. The slogans basically write themselves, and the two classics are “Love All” and “She Found Her Match.”

Put them on sashes, visors, cake toppers or matching tees. Dress the bride in white (obviously) and the crew in pink or green. Swap the trivia questions for how-well-do-you-know-the-couple, and turn the trophy toss into a round of “who’s catching the bouquet.”

And because it’s a bachelorette, the gift bag can get a little cheekier than it would at a birthday brunch with the in-laws. Which brings us to the good part.
