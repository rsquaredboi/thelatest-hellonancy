Why a game beats a dinner reservation

Dinner is lovely. Dinner is also two people facing each other across a table, recapping the week. A game puts you on the same side of the net, trying something new and almost definitely laughing at some point.

That’s the genius of a tennis-themed set. The theme does the icebreaking for you. “Want to play tennis?” is a much easier sentence to say out loud than whatever you were rehearsing in the shower.

It’s already code on TikTok, where couples call it “playing tennis” (plenty of the creators saying it are Nancy’s own paid partners, to be clear). One husband’s “morning routine” video passed a million views. We’ll let you guess what the routine involved.

And tennis vocabulary is basically a flirting cheat sheet. Love means zero, so every game starts at love-love. An ace is a serve nobody touches. A smash is the overhead power shot that ends the point. You’re welcome for your new pillow talk.

Also, consider the economics. A nice dinner for two is gone by the time the check arrives. A doubles set sticks around for a lot of rematches.

How to play doubles

No rulebook required. A few house rules help, though.

  1. Take turns serving. One of you picks the toy, the other picks the mode. Next game, switch sides.
  2. Start on the lowest setting. Every match has a warm-up. Work up together and stop wherever feels best.
  3. Talk about what feels good. Out loud, in the moment. “More of that” is a complete sentence.
  4. Keep score as a joke. Love-fifteen. Deuce. Advantage, you. Nobody’s really counting. Probably.
  5. No pressure. Some nights are a five-set epic. Some are a quick hit and an early night. Both count.
